Disease brief 04 — Intestinal autoimmunity

The diet works in principle.
In practice, it leaks.

01 — Executive summary

The conclusion first

The diet is effective but incomplete. The question is not whether a drug replaces the diet, but whether an adjunct can protect the gut from unavoidable trace exposure.

01

Any pipeline interpretation outside that premise overstates the case.

03

The real question

Not “does a drug replace the diet” but “can an adjunct protect the intestine from unavoidable trace gluten”.

02 — Disease

From gluten to villous atrophy

Celiac disease is an immune response to dietary gluten that destroys intestinal villi. A clear mechanism is the starting point for target development.

Genetics are necessary, not sufficient

More than 93% of patients carry HLA-DQ2, most of the rest DQ8 — yet 30–40% of the general population carries the same types.

Classic presentation is the minority

Overt malabsorption is rare. Non-classic presentations dominate: fatigue, iron-deficiency anaemia, osteoporosis, dermatitis herpetiformis, infertility, neuropathy.

41.4%

Asymptomatic at biopsy-proven diagnosis

Being symptom-free at detection does not mean the disease is mild.

04 — Severity

Uncommon, but not negligible

Adult diagnosis: haematological malignancy SMR 4.77; beyond 15 years after diagnosis, all malignancies decline.

Refractory celiac disease (RCD)

Under 1% of celiac disease; in a high-prevalence study, 0.31% of diagnosed patients and 0.002% of the general population. Type I (68%) has a better prognosis; type II (23%) carries clonal aberrant IELs and a high risk of enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma.

Distinguish non-responsive disease

Non-responsive celiac disease is common and usually reflects ongoing gluten exposure, an alternative diagnosis or slow healing. Do not fold it into RCD.

05 — Current treatment

The diet works — incompletely

Even on a strict diet, trace exposure and incomplete mucosal healing persist. That gap is the rationale for adjunctive therapy.

The paediatric screening paradox

Screening at age 7 finds 1% prevalence, yet 90% remain undiagnosed. UK cohorts missed 83–91%, disproportionately among deprived children.

08 — Investor view

Six reasons, eight risks

The opportunity sits in the diagnostic gap and a measurable histological endpoint; the risk sits in repeated failures and the safety bar of a non-fatal disease.

Why to engage

  • 1.5% seroprevalence with more than 80% undiagnosed
  • HLA stratification and serological staging are available
  • Objective histological endpoint — villus-to-crypt ratio plus symptoms
  • An FDA adjunct-to-diet framework already exists
  • Phase 2 IL-15 and CD122 signals in 2026
  • Definable high-need segments: refractory and non-responsive disease

Risks

  • The diet is burdensome but effective for many
  • Histology is variable and requires biopsy
  • Symptom endpoints are subjective
  • Enzyme and tolerance approaches have failed repeatedly
  • TG2 phase 2b missed its primary endpoint
  • Immune drugs carry a large safety trade-off in a non-fatal disease
  • Both histology and symptoms are needed
  • Korea and East Asia are low-prevalence launch geographies
